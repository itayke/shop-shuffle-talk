How Shop Shuffle looks and sounds, for the talk deck. Every image is a real frame from the game.

The street

One street, always daytime

The big screen is a side-on street: sky, a hazy skyline, the sidewalk where shops stand, and the road where customers cross. It stays in daylight whatever the page theme. Frames below are from a round with 16 bot phones, at 1920×1080 with the presenter bar cropped off.

Shops

Ten storefronts, any color

Every shop is drawn in code from parts (sign, awning, wall, window, door), so a name typed on a phone becomes a storefront live. Ten designs, each in a player's own tint; three tints shown.

  • A 32-pixel columnOne shop is one column wide and one floor tall; stacked shops touch.
  • Three shades a tintLight, base and dark of one hue, the hues spread so neighbors never match.
  • Ink outlinesA dark ink line around everything: shops, legs, people, clouds.
  • Thin walking legsTwo segments, knees out. They fold away when a shop sits.
  • Tiny signsNames in a 3×5 pixel font, two short lines at most.
  • Loud underwearShorts in tacky dots or stripes, picked from the shop's name.

Color

Two palettes

Screen chrome (bars, panels, buttons) follows light and dark mode. The street never does: it keeps its own daylight palette.

Light and dark halves. The amber accent is the title and the primary button.

Type

Three faces

All three are on Google Fonts. The pixel faces stay crisp only at whole multiples of their grid: Jersey 15 at 27 px per pixel step, Press Start 2P at 8.

Jersey 15Titles and banners. Drawn hard-edged with a round dark outline (pixel-title.js).

Work SansEverything people read: lists, cards, body text. 400, 500, 600.
16 shops open. Scan the code and name your shop: it drops onto the street.

Sound

8-bit, no music

The game's own synth, the same code: NES-style pulse, triangle and noise channels, built in the browser with no audio files. Click any pad.

Ideas for the deck

Suggestions only. A sound on every slide gets old fast; two or three moments is plenty.
